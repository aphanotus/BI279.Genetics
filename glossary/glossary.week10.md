# BI279 Glossary

## Week 10: Sex chromosomes; recombination; mapping

- **Complementation Analysis (Test)**: Crosses used to determine if two independent mutations causing a similar phenotype are alleles of the same gene.
- **Complementation**: Occurs when crossing two homozygous mutants yields a wild-type phenotype in the F1 progeny, indicating the mutations are in different genes.
- **Complementation Group**: A group of mutant strains that fail to complement one another; all members of the group are presumed to harbor mutations in the same gene.
- **Allelic Mutations**: Two mutations that cause a similar phenotype and reside in the same gene.
- **Maternal Effect Inheritance**: An inheritance pattern where the offspring’s phenotype is determined by the genotype of the mother, regardless of the offspring's own genotype.
- **Maternal mRNA**: Cytoplasmic mRNA loaded into the oocyte (egg cell) that quickly produces protein after fertilization, driving early embryonic phenotypes.
- **Sex-Limited Trait (Sex Limitation)**: A trait where the expression of the phenotype is restricted to only one sex. (Loci responsible for sex-limited traits may be linked to autosomes or sex chromosomes.)
- **Sex-Influenced Trait (Sex Influence)**: A trait where the phenotype is modulated or influenced by the sex of the individual. (Loci responsible for sex-influenced traits may be linked to autosomes or sex chromosomes.)
- **Autosome**: Chromosomes present in the same number in males and females.
- **X-linkage genes**: Genes located on the X chromosome that exhibit inheritance patterns distinct from autosomal genes.
- **Y-linked Genes**: Genes located only on the Y chromosome, typically involved in male sex determination or male-limited traits.
- **Hemizygous**: Describes the genotype of a male (or any individual) possessing only one copy of a gene (e.g., all X-linked genes in human XY males).
- **Homogametic**: The sex that produces gametes carrying only one type of sex chromosome (e.g., human females, XX; bird males, ZZ).
- **Heterogametic**: The sex that produces gametes carrying two types of sex chromosomes (e.g., human males, XY; bird females, ZW).
- **Reciprocal Cross**: A pair of crosses used to test the influence of parental sex on offspring phenotypes; often shows different outcomes for sex-linked genes.
- **SRY Gene**: The sex-determining region of the Y-chromosome; a key sex-determination gene in mammals.
- **Pseudoautosomal Regions (PAR)**: Regions of homology shared between the X and Y chromosomes that allow them to pair during meiosis.
- **Isogametic**: describes organisms or reproductive processes that produce gametes (sex cells) that are identical in size, shape, and structure. In isogametic reproduction, haploid cells cannot be visually classified as "male" (small) or "female" (large), and are simply referred to as different mating types.
- **Thomas Hunt Morgan:** American geneticist who developed fruit flies as a genetic model organism. Morgan led a large and productive research group that discovered X-linkage and concluded that linked genes are arranged linearly on chromosomes.
- **Nettie Stevens**: Morgan's student who discovered X and Y sex chromosomes in a beetle in 1905.
