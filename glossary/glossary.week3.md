# BI279 Glossary

## Week 3: Cellular DNA replication; telomeres

- **Genome language model**: An artificial intelligence model trained on large biological sequence datasets (DNA, RNA, or protein) using self-supervised techniques adapted from natural language processing. By treating nucleotide or amino acid sequences as a formal language, the model learns the underlying regulatory "grammar" and structural syntax of genomes. This enables automated predictions of gene expression, variant effects, and functional elements across diverse organisms without relying on prior sequence annotation.
- **Replication Fork**: The Y-shaped structure formed when the DNA double helix unwinds during replication, where new DNA strands are synthesized.
- **Origin of Replication**: the DNA sequence on a chromosome where DNA replication begins and proceeding bidirectional.
- **DnaA**: The protein that binds to the origin of replication and initiates the unwinding of the DNA helix.
- **Helicase**: The protein that unwinds double-stranded DNA into single strands by breaking the hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs, allowing a replication fork to proceed along a chromosome.
- **Single-stranded binding proteins**: Proteins that stabilize the un-paired DNA strands during DNA replication.
- **DNA topoisomerase**: Separating the two DNA strands at a replication fork creates upstream torque or supercoiling. Topoisomerases are enzymes that relieve DNA supercoiling by cutting the DNA phosphodiester backbone, allowing it to relax, then reconnecting it.
- **Primase**: To elongate a DNA strand, DNA polymerase III requires a primer with a free 3'-OH group. Primase synthesizes a short RNA primer (5-15 bp) on the template strand, providing the free 3'-OH.
- **RNA Primer**: A short RNA molecule, synthesized by primase, that provides a free 3'-hydroxyl group for DNA polymerase to begin synthesizing a new DNA strand.
- **DNA Polymerase III**: The main enzyme that synthesizes new DNA strands by adding nucleotides to the 3' end of a growing chain, using a template strand [Simons 105, Fowler et al. 68]. It also proofreads for accuracy.
- **Leading Strand**: During DNA replication, the new strand that is synthesized continuously in the 5' to 3' direction, moving towards the replication fork. Because its template orientation matches the direction of fork unzipping, it requires only a single initial RNA primer for complete synthesis.
- **Lagging Strand**: During DNA replication, the new strand that is synthesized discontinuously in short fragments (Okazaki fragments) in the 5' to 3' direction, moving away from the replication fork. Because its template is oriented antiparallel to the progress of the replication fork, the lagging strand requires repeated RNA priming and subsequent ligation of DNA fragments to produce a continuous strand.
- **Okazaki fragments**: Short, newly synthesized DNA segments formed on the lagging strand during semidiscontinuous DNA replication. Because DNA polymerase can only synthesize in the 5' to 3' direction, the lagging template must be copied discontinuously in brief bursts moving away from the  replication fork. Each fragment begins with a short RNA primer, created by RNA primase, and is ultimately joined to adjacent fragments by DNA ligase to produce a continuous strand.
- **Tsuneko and Reiji Okazaki**: A pioneering husband-and-wife molecular biology team best known for discovering Okazaki fragments in 1968, solving the fundamental mechanics of lagging strand DNA synthesis. Tragically, Reiji died in 1975 at age 44 from leukemia caused by his childhood exposure to the atomic bombing of Hiroshima. Tsuneko continued their research independently, going on to head her own laboratory at Nagoya University where she remains a leader in molecular biology.
- **DNA polymerase I**: The DNA polymerase variant specialized for removing RNA primer sequences and replacing them with DNA nucleotides at the conjunctions between Okazaki fragments.
- **DNA Ligase**: An enzyme that seals nicks in the phosphodiester backbone of DNA, for example, joining Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand.
- **Concurrent synthesis**: The simultaneous, coordinated replication of both the leading and lagging strands by a protein complex at a single replication fork. By looping the lagging strand template, the complex enables two connected DNA polymerase enzymes to move together in the same overall physical direction down the DNA molecule. This structural coupling ensures that continuous leading-strand and discontinuous lagging-strand synthesis occur at the same time.
- **Telomere**: Repetitive DNA sequences found at the ends of linear eukaryotic chromosomes, which protect the chromosome ends and do not code for a particular gene.
- **Telomerase**: An enzyme, active in germ and stem cells (and some cancer cells), that uses an internal RNA template to extend the 3' end of the parental DNA strand at telomeres, counteracting chromosome shortening during replication.
- **Telomere shortening:** The progressive loss of DNA at the terminal ends of linear eukaryotic chromosomes during successive rounds of replication. It is driven primarily by the end-replication problem, where lagging-strand synthesis cannot replace the terminal RNA primer at the 5' end. Over time, telomere attrition limits a cell's replicative lifespan, triggering  senescence or cell death unless actively counteracted by telomerase.
